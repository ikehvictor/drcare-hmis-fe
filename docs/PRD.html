Overview

The problem, the idea, and what success looks like.

Problem

Hospitals in our market run very differently. A small clinic sends patients from Records straight to the Cashier and the doctor; a larger hospital adds reception, triage and HMO patients who skip the cashier. Each has its own paper card numbers and registration forms. Off-the-shelf systems force one way of working, and custom builds are slow and expensive to change.

Idea

One core platform with plug-in modules. Every hospital gets the same engine but switches modules on or off, shapes its own forms, numbering and patient journey, and runs its own administration. Dr. Care oversees all hospitals from a separate platform console.

Success looks like

  • Two very different hospitals register patients with their own forms and numbers on the same build, with no code change.
  • A hospital admin sets up branches, staff and access without calling Dr. Care.
  • A cashier runs a full shift and reconciles it, and the owner trusts the daily report.
  • Every sensitive change can be traced: who, what, when, where, before and after.

Who it's for

Four layers of authority. A person can only do something when every layer above allows it.

Dr. Care platform team

Onboards hospitals, decides which modules each may use and sets limits on branches and users. Support access is logged and visible to the hospital.

Hospital Super Admin

Runs the hospital's own setup: branches, staff accounts, roles, modules, numbering, forms and settings.

Records clerk / front desk

Finds returning patients and registers new ones at their branch.

Cashiers, nurses, doctors and others

Work at service points with what their roles allow. Cash Point and clinical modules come in later stages.

Rule of inheritance: Dr. Care entitles → Hospital enables and configures → Branch overrides → Role permits → Person acts.

Principles

Decisions every feature follows.

  • Configuration over code. Forms, number formats, prices, payment rules and patient flow are data the admin edits.
  • Modules talk through events. Records, Cash Point, Lab and others never reach into each other. Switching one off breaks nothing else.
  • Always at least one branch. A single clinic is modelled as one branch, so opening a second site needs no rework.
  • Nothing clinical or financial is deleted. Records are deactivated with a reason; money mistakes are fixed with reversing entries.
  • Everything sensitive is audited, and sensitive actions can require a second approver.
  • Software never blocks care. Every patient journey allows "treat first, pay later" with a reason that's logged.

Hospital admin

Where the Hospital Super Admin sets the hospital up. Lives at /admin.

Branches & service points

Built
  • A1Add and edit branches with name, code, address and phone; one branch is the main branch.
  • A2Branches can't exceed the plan's limit; the main branch can't be deactivated.
  • A3Each branch has service points (Reception, Records, Cashier, Triage, Consultation, Lab, Pharmacy, Radiology) that can be opened or closed.

Staff accounts

Built
  • A4The admin creates an account with name, email, phone, job title, staff category and specialty, roles and branch access. It works immediately; no invitation is sent.
  • A5The admin sets the first password. It must meet the hospital's minimum length, mix letters and numbers, and not contain the person's email name. By default the person must choose their own password at first sign-in.
  • A6Passwords are never stored in readable form or written to the audit log; the log only records that a password was set.
  • A7The admin can set a new password for someone else, suspend and reactivate accounts. Nobody can suspend themselves or change their own access.
  • A8Branch access is all branches or specific ones; a cashier limited to the annex can't work at main.
  • A9Suspended accounts don't count towards the plan's user limit.

Staff categories

Built
  • A10The hospital defines its kinds of staff, such as Doctor, Nurse, Records & front desk, Cashier & accounts and Administration.
  • A11Each category can list specialties (for example Cardiology, Paediatrics, Midwifery) and suggest roles that are ticked when creating an account in it.
  • A12A category can't be deleted while anyone is in it.

Roles & functions

Built
  • A13A function is one thing a person may do, such as "Register new patients" or "Issue refunds". Modules declare the functions the system enforces.
  • A14A role is a named group of functions, such as Head Nurse or Cardiology Doctor. Admins create, copy, edit and delete roles; a role in use can't be deleted.
  • A15The Hospital Super Admin role always has every function and can't be changed, so a hospital can't lock itself out.
  • A16Hospitals can name their own functions, such as "Approve the nurses' duty roster". They work in roles and on people like any other, but are marked not enforced yet until a module supports them.
  • A17Per-person access: any function can be given to one person or taken away from them, without changing the role for anyone else. The page shows where each function comes from.
  • A18Functions of a switched-off module don't count.

Modules

Built
  • A19The admin switches on or off the modules on the hospital's plan. Dependencies switch together (Cash Point needs Medical Records), with a warning first.
  • A20Each module has options. Medical Records: phone required, estimated age allowed, capture next of kin, duplicate check. Cash Point: payment methods, part payments, deposits, refund approval amount.
  • A21Modules not on the plan can be requested from Dr. Care.

Patient numbering

Built
  • A22The hospital builds its format from tokens: {PREFIX}, {BR} branch, {YYYY}/{YY} year, {MM} month, {CAT} category, {SEQ:n} running number. Examples: LKS/000123, LKA-26-04571, 0123/26.
  • A23Counting can be hospital-wide, per branch or per category, and can restart yearly or monthly. The format is rejected if it could repeat numbers.
  • A24A starting number lets a hospital continue from its paper records. Old card numbers can be kept and searched.
  • A25Changing a saved format asks first and shows numbers before and after.

Registration forms & patient categories

Built
  • A26Core fields every record needs (names, sex, date of birth, phone) can be reordered but not removed.
  • A27Admins add fields from a standard library (address, next of kin, blood group, NIN…) or invent custom fields of any type, arrange them in sections and mark them required.
  • A28Patient categories (General, Child, Antenatal, Corporate…) each use a form and have a code used in numbers. A form in use can't be deleted.
  • A29A live preview shows the form as the clerk will see it, with Medical Records options applied.

Settings

Built
  • A30Hospital profile: name, short name, contact details, logo, date format.
  • A31Security: sign out after inactivity, quick PIN switch for shared computers, two-step sign-in, minimum password length.
  • A32Sensitive actions (merge patients, refunds, voids, discounts, user and role changes) can require approval by a chosen role.
  • A33A branch can override the sign-out time and PIN switch; otherwise it uses the hospital setting.

Audit log

Built
  • A34Every change records who, what, when, where (branch), how (which computer) and the values before and after. Sensitive entries are flagged.
  • A35Entries can never be edited or removed. Filter by category, person, date, text or sensitive only.
  • A36Export to CSV; the export itself is logged. Text that could run as a spreadsheet formula is made safe.

Front desk

Where records clerks work. Lives at /desk, separate from the admin.

Access to the desk

Built
  • D1Only active staff whose access includes "Register new patients" can use the desk, and only at branches they may work at.
  • D2While Medical Records is switched off, the desk explains why and blocks registration and search. Registration also waits until a number format is saved and a patient category is in use.

Register a patient

Built
  • D3The clerk picks a patient category, which loads that category's form with Medical Records options applied.
  • D4Required fields, phone numbers, emails and dates are checked. A patient who doesn't know their date of birth can give an estimated age, if allowed.
  • D5The patient number is issued from the admin's format and counting rules when the record is saved. Numbers never repeat, even if the admin later changes the format.
  • D6Duplicate check: same name, date of birth and phone (ignoring spacing, capitals and +234 vs 0) shows a possible match. The clerk opens the existing record or confirms a new patient; the override is logged.
  • D7Each registration writes a sensitive audit entry with the number, category, name, sex, date of birth, phone and branch. Other answers stay on the record only.

Find a patient & record view

Built
  • D8Search by patient number, name, phone, and old card number when the hospital keeps them. Exact number matches come first.
  • D9The record shows a summary (age, date of birth, phone, category, where and by whom registered) and every answer grouped as on the form.

Next: Cash Point

Billing and payments. Not started; requirements from the architecture plan.

  • C1Service catalogue and price lists per payer (cash, HMO, corporate, staff) and per branch, with effective dates.
  • C2Any module can raise a charge; charges group into a bill per visit showing paid and outstanding.
  • C3Payments by cash, POS, transfer or deposit; part payments if allowed; numbered receipts, with reprints marked COPY.
  • C4Till sessions: the cashier opens with a float and closes with a count; the system shows over or short.
  • C5Refunds, voids, discounts and waivers never edit the original. They create reversing records and need approval above a set amount.
  • C6Reports from day one: collections by cashier, method, service and branch; outstanding bills; refunds and discounts; till over/short.

Quality & security

Requirements that apply to every feature.

  • Q1Each hospital's data is isolated from every other's, enforced in the database.
  • Q2Strong passwords, optional two-step sign-in, automatic sign-out on shared front-desk computers.
  • Q3Patient data encrypted in transit and at rest; compliance with local privacy law such as Nigeria's NDPA.
  • Q4Money and audit records are append-only; amounts use exact decimal arithmetic with a currency.
  • Q5Works on phones and tablets as well as desktops, with keyboard and screen-reader support.
  • Q6Copes with unreliable internet: clear offline messages and retries first, full offline mode later.
  • Q7Data shapes ready for ICD-10 diagnoses and HL7 FHIR export.

How it's built

For engineering colleagues.

  • Front end (this repository, drcare-hmis-fe): Next.js 16 and TypeScript, Tailwind CSS with Dr. Care brand colours.
  • Demo data is kept in the browser so reviewers can click through every flow. "Reset demo data" in the account menu starts over.
  • Automated tests: 156 browser tests cover every flow above, on desktop and phone sizes.
  • Planned backend (separate repository): a modular monolith, likely NestJS with PostgreSQL, Redis for jobs and live queues, and object storage for documents.
  • The full technical blueprint is in docs/ARCHITECTURE.md in the repository.

Open decisions

Questions for the team before the backend is built.

  • Which countries and currencies at launch? This affects tax, privacy law, ID formats and where data is hosted.
  • How early do Records and Cash Point need to work with no internet?
  • Pricing model: per module, per branch or per user?
  • Hosting: our cloud only, or also on-premise for large or government hospitals?
  • Who sets up new hospitals: our team, the hospital admin, or a guided setup wizard?
  • Confirm the backend stack (NestJS and PostgreSQL) or an existing team preference.
  • Should the hospital's own functions ever be enforced, for example by linking them to a module's screen?

Glossary

TenantOne hospital or organisation using Dr. Care; its data is isolated from others.
BranchA physical site of a hospital. Every hospital has at least one.
Service pointA desk or room where patients are attended to: Records, Cashier, OPD room…
ModuleA plug-in feature area such as Medical Records or Cash Point.
FunctionOne thing a person may do, like "Issue refunds". Also called a permission.
RoleA named group of functions given to staff, like "Records Officer".
Staff categoryA kind of staff, like Doctor or Nurse, with optional specialties.
Patient categoryA kind of patient, like Antenatal, which decides the registration form.
VisitOne trip a patient makes to the hospital.
Audit logThe permanent record of who changed what, when and where.
